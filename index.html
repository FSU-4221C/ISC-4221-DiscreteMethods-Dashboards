    The eight modules

    Modules follow the order they are taught. Topic numbers match COURSE_TOPIC_MAP.md, so 3.5.5 here is 3.5.5 in the slides, the labs, and the exams.

    • Module 0

      Foundations & Tooling

      Git and the GitHub flow, Python environments with uv, loops and recursion, NumPy vectorization, and plotting. The entry point for everything else.

      12 topics · Labs 01–02 · no slide deck

    • Module 1

      Algorithm Design & Analysis

      Brute force, divide and conquer, greedy strategies, and the vocabulary of efficiency: counting operations, Big-O, and the complexity classes.

      24 topics · 41 slide frames · Labs 03–04 · midterm

    • Module 2

      Probability & Random Processes

      Sample spaces and conditional probability, pseudo-random generators, distributions, the Central Limit Theorem, Monte Carlo methods, and random walks.

      51 topics · 84 slide frames · Labs 05–06 · midterm

    • Module 3

      Graphs

      From Königsberg to Kruskal: representations, connectivity, depth- and breadth-first traversal, Dijkstra, spanning trees, and the travelling salesman.

      55 topics · 115 slide frames · Labs 07–08 · midterm

    • Module 4

      Image Processing

      Pixels and file formats, histograms and contrast, convolution kernels, noise and filtering, edge detection, segmentation, and morphology.

      51 topics · 138 slide frames · Lab 09 · final

    • Module 5

      Data Mining

      Clustering by linkage and by k-means, Voronoi diagrams and centroidal tessellation, decision trees, impurity measures, and information gain.

      49 topics · 137 slide frames · Labs 10–11 · final

    • Module 6

      Computational Geometry

      Points, lines and the dot product; triangle orientation and area; polygon triangulation by ear clipping; convex hulls; Delaunay and quadrature.

      45 topics · 123 slide frames · Labs 12–13 · final

    • Module 7

      Discrete Optimization

      Formulating a linear program, the geometry of the feasible region, slack variables and basic feasible solutions, and the simplex method tableau by tableau.

      36 topics · 121 slide frames · Lab 14

    Cross-cutting threads

    Five ideas are taught more than once, in different modules, under different names. Following a thread is often the fastest way to understand any one of its appearances.

    • Thread

      k-means everywhere

      The same algorithm, three names. It quantizes colour in M4 §4.3, clusters points in M5 §5.4, and produces a centroidal Voronoi tessellation in M5 §5.5. Assign each point to its nearest centre; move each centre to the mean of what it owns; repeat.

      Modules 4, 5, 6

    • Thread

      Greedy algorithms gallery

      Take the locally best step and never reconsider it. Coin change in M1 §1.3, Dijkstra and Kruskal in M3 §3.5 and §3.6, and Hunt’s tree construction in M5 §5.6. Two of them are provably optimal, one is not — and knowing which is the exam question.

      Modules 1, 3, 5 · includes where it fails

    • Thread

      Delaunay triangulation

      Taught as the dual of the Voronoi diagram in M5 §5.5 and as a triangulation method in M6 §6.5, then used in Lab 13 to build a continuous field from scattered sensor readings.

      Modules 5, 6 · Lab 13

    • Thread

      Algorithm complexity comparator

      Big-O is introduced in M1 §1.4 and then quietly used everywhere: comparing graph representations in M3 §3.2, costing Lloyd’s method in M5 §5.4, and explaining why brute-force LP is hopeless in M7 §7.4.

      Modules 1, 3, 5, 7

    Our accessibility commitment

    Every figure here has words

    Every chart, animation, algorithm trace and graph drawing on this dashboard ships with a real data table of the numbers behind it and a text summary that updates whenever the figure does. Not an alt string — the actual numbers. If a figure ever appears without them, that is a defect, and we want to hear about it.

    This dashboard targets WCAG 2.1 Level AA plus the 2.2 AA additions. In practice:

    • Keyboard, end to end. Nothing needs a mouse. Every control, every step-through, every scrollable table is reachable with Tab and operable with arrows, and the focus indicator is always visible.
    • Colour is never the only signal. Every series, state and verdict also carries a word, a shape, or a dash pattern.
    • Nothing moves without permission. Animations never start on their own, every one has a pause control, and prefers-reduced-motion is honoured.
    • Measured contrast. Body text runs at 14.28:1 on white, headings in FSU Garnet at 9.21:1, links in Plaza Brick at 11.87:1 — all against a 4.5:1 requirement, all computed rather than estimated.
    • It reflows. Usable at 320 CSS px and at 400% zoom, with no two-dimensional scrolling.
    • It works offline. No fonts, scripts, images or data are fetched from anywhere. Copy the folder to a USB stick and it still works.
